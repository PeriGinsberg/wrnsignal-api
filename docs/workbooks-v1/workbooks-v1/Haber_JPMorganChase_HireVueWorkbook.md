## Section 2. How the JPMorganChase HireVue Works

There is no person on the other side. You see a question on screen, get a short time to think, then record your answer against a timer.

What reported JPMC Summer Analyst HireVues have looked like (IGotAnOffer analysis of Glassdoor reports, updated September 2026):
* 3 to 5 questions
* 30 to 120 seconds to think before each one
* 1.5 to 2 minutes to record each answer
* 1 optional retry per question

**Plan for the hardest version:** 30 seconds to think and 90 seconds to answer. If you get more time, that is a bonus.

**About the retry:** use it only if something actually went wrong (you lost your place, you got cut off, you went blank). Do not retry just because it felt imperfect. Your second take is not guaranteed to be better, and you do not get a third.

**How it is scored:** JPMorgan does not publicly say how much is AI versus human review. One published guide reports the scoring looks at what you say and how you sound, not your face (The Interview Guys, 2026). Either way, the words matter most. Clear answers with real examples win.

COACH NOTE: All format details are sourced. Do not add claims about what "most candidates" see. The posting itself says the HireVue is her chance to "bring your resume to life," which is the framing to reinforce.

## Section 5. Prompt 1: Tell Me About Yourself

**Why it's here:** Reported as one of the most frequent JPMC Summer Analyst HireVue questions (IGotAnOffer, Glassdoor data). Even if it doesn't come up word for word, this answer is the backbone of "walk me through your resume" and "why are you the right candidate."

**How to build it: Present, Past, Future**
* **Present:** who you are right now. School, major, what you are focused on.
* **Past:** two or three things that got you here. Pick, don't list. Not every internship gets equal time.
* **Future:** what you are looking for now and why it points at this program. This part quietly answers "why MLDP" before they ask.

Target: about 60 to 75 seconds. Short is a strength here.

STARTER:

I got into marketing through photography. I started my own business shooting yachts, events, and weddings, and I also shoot shelter dogs for their adoption listings. That's where I figured out that the picture is only half of it, and getting it in front of the right people is the other half. Since then I've done three marketing internships. This past summer I was with the Sport Fishing Championship, a tournament series that streams on ESPN+, where I found and pitched sponsors, built a way to track our weekly email numbers, and worked on site at a tournament in Newport.

What I'm looking for now is a place where I can learn the bigger version of that, the creative side and the numbers side together, at a brand people actually know. That's what drew me to MLDP."

## Section 9. Prompt 5: Changing How You Communicate for Your Audience

**Why it's here:** "Tell me about a time where you had to change your communications strategy to better fit your audience" was reported from a JPMC Summer Analyst HireVue on Glassdoor. It also matches the posting's "strong verbal and written communication skills."

**Your story: The Psychology Project.** You already have it. The point of this version is not that you organized the group. It is that you thought about how it would be heard.

**Reminder: STAR + E**
* **S**ituation: set it up in one or two sentences
* **T**ask: what was on you
* **A**ction: what you did, in "I," with the most time here
* **R**esult: what happened
* **E**motion: a moment the listener can relate to (every group project has one)
* Don't announce the trait. Let the story show it.

STARTER:
"I had a group project in a psychology class, and a few weeks in it was drifting. Nobody had picked up the plan, and deadlines were getting close.

I knew if I just told everyone what to do, it would land badly. Nobody likes being managed by someone in their own class. So I didn't point out that work wasn't getting done. I built a simple plan with who does what and when, and I brought it to the group as a way to make things easier for everyone, which it was. People got to stop wondering what they were supposed to be doing.

Everyone did their part, we got an A, and we all stayed on good terms. What I took from it is that how you say something matters as much as whether you're right."

## Section 12. Recording Day Checklist

* Laptop at eye level. Camera slightly above your eyes, not looking up your nose.
* Light in front of you, not behind you. A window behind you turns you into a silhouette.
* Quiet room, door closed, phone on silent and out of reach.
* Plain background.
* Dress like it's a real interview, top to bottom.
* Look at the camera lens when you talk, not at your own face on screen.
* Use the 30 seconds to pick your story and your first sentence. That's it.
* Start with your headline. End on purpose. Stop when you're done; don't fill time.
* Do the HireVue practice question first if they offer one.
* Before you start, check your time zone against any deadline in the invite.
