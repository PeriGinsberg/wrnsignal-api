01

SIGNAL is a decision engine first and a toolkit second

Most job-search products help you produce more applications. SIGNAL is built on the opposite premise: the problem is not volume, it is targeting, and the first thing it sells is permission to stop.

A user pastes a resume and a job description. In about thirty seconds SIGNAL returns a verdict of Priority Apply, Apply, Review or Pass, plus a 0–100 score, the specific evidence in their background that makes them competitive, the specific gaps that will get them screened out, and one concrete next step. That verdict is produced by a deterministic scoring engine, not by asking a language model what it thinks.

Everything else in the product hangs off that decision. Once a job is worth pursuing, SIGNAL rewrites how the user presents themselves for that job, drafts the letter, works the contacts who can get a human to read it, and tracks the whole campaign until something lands. When a round is booked, it generates the questions that interview will actually contain and drafts an answer to each one out of the user's own experience.

The pitch to a college student or early-career job seeker is not "apply faster." It is that you are sending thirty applications and getting two callbacks, and that is a targeting problem you can see and fix. Three words: Stop applying blind.

02

The loop a user actually runs

Four moves, repeated per job. Every function in the product belongs to one of them, which makes this the natural spine for messaging as well as for onboarding.

Move one
Decide

Is this job worth a Saturday? Score it before you write a word.

Move two
Compete

Reposition the resume for this job, then draft the letter.

Move three
Work it

Track the application, work your contacts for a referral, hit the follow-ups on time.

Move four
Convert

Walk into each round knowing the questions and your answers to them.

Why this framing sells

Competitors own one move each: a resume tool, a tracker, a networking scraper. SIGNAL is the only one of them that starts with the decision, and the decision is what makes the other three moves cheap. A user who scores ten jobs and applies to three has already saved more time than any resume optimizer can give back.

03

The nine functions

Grouped by the move they serve. Each carries a status pill saying where it runs, because the web app and the iOS app do not carry the same set, and a campaign pointed at the wrong one promises a smaller product than it means to.

Move one: Decide

The wedge, and the whole free experience
F01
Live · free

The Free Scan

Watch a recruiter read your resume for one specific job: no account, no card, about thirty seconds.

What the user does
  • Uploads a PDF/DOCX resume or pastes the text
  • Pastes the full job description
  • Hits run: no signup gate in front of the result
What SIGNAL gives back
  • A verdict and score: Priority Apply / Apply / Review / Pass
  • Why you're competitive: bullets that name the actual employer, role or metric on their resume and tie it to the posting's own language
  • Risks before you apply: the specific gaps, rated low/medium/high, never invented
  • A marked-up read of the resume: lines a recruiter would underline or circle, with margin notes, plus a one-line verdict and a named next step
  • Locked previews of the paid functions
The free scan is a complete, honest product moment, not a teaser, and that is the acquisition asset. "Real verdict. Real strengths. Real risks. Real next step." The upgrade is not "see your result," it is "now do something about it, and do it for every job."
F02
Live · paid

JobFit: the full scoring engine

The same verdict, but run against a profile that knows your targets, your constraints and your real resume, and saved.

What changes when you pay
  • Scores against a structured profile, not a guess from pasted text: target roles, locations, timeline, job type, hard nos, constraints
  • Scores against the persona (resume version) you choose
  • Unlimited jobs, each one saved and re-openable
  • Every scored job auto-creates a tracker entry so scoring and tracking are one action
What the result contains
  • Decision, score, and the evidence behind both
  • Hard gates when the job is structurally wrong: credential required, wrong grad window, remote/in-person mismatch, seniority, part-time, government or heavy-analytics requirements
  • Location constraint read straight from the posting
  • A strategy hand-off into positioning and the cover letter, both anchored on the same lead signal
The commercial argument for the paid tier is repeatability: one scan tells you about one job, a profile tells you about your search. Ninety-nine dollars buys a working method, not a document.

Move two: Compete

Two artifacts, both generated from the same verdict
F03
Live · paid

Resume Positioning

Not a rewrite. A reordering and relabeling of what you have already done, in this job's language.

What it produces
  • A role angle: which of seven stances to take for this job (client-facing, analytical, creative, technical, operations, leadership, research)
  • An explicit reorder plan of lead with, support with, then include and de-emphasize, each with the reason and the action
  • A summary statement recommendation, including a drafted one if the resume needs it
  • One to six before/after bullet edits with the reason each matters
The trust mechanic
  • Every "before" line must be a verbatim substring of the user's own resume: the system rejects any edit that isn't, so it structurally cannot invent experience
  • Missing keywords are found deterministically first, then handed to the model as fact
  • Whether a summary section already exists is detected, not guessed
This is the strongest honesty claim in the product and it is under-marketed today. "Rewrites in this job's language that surface what you've already done. Nothing invented", and the architecture actually enforces it.
F04
Live · paid

Cover Letter

A letter that opens on your strongest proof and names the gap instead of hiding it.

How it is built
  • Takes the JobFit verdict and the positioning angle as input, so the letter and the resume order argue the same thing
  • Opens on a named experience or metric chosen by the engine, not a generic hook
  • Explicitly addresses the top risk when one exists
  • Ships with a formatted contact header, ready to send
What the user gets
  • A complete draft, cached so revisiting the job is instant
  • A stated tone, so it reads like a person and not a template
  • Nothing generated at all on a Pass: the product refuses to help you apply to a job it told you to skip
The refusal-on-Pass behavior is a genuinely differentiating detail worth putting in copy: a product with the discipline to say no is a product whose yes means something.

The raw material

What the engine reads about you
F05
Live · paid

Profile & Personas

One setup that makes every future scan sharper, and up to ten resumes for as many different directions.

What the user sets up
  • Direction: job type (full-time / internship / both), target roles, preferred locations, timeline
  • Situation: school, major, graduation year, current status
  • Judgment: strongest skills, biggest concern, hard nos, constraints, openness to entry level
  • Evidence: the resume itself, plus optional writing samples and free-text context
Personas
  • Up to ten named resume versions, for example "Marketing" and "Operations", each scored independently
  • The tracker records which resume was used for every application
  • Resume upload parses PDF, DOCX or plain text
Personas are the honest answer to "I'm applying to two kinds of jobs and my resume is fighting itself." The cap of ten sits well past what any individual realistically runs, so it reads as generous rather than metered. Worth saying out loud in copy, because most competitors meter exactly this.

Moves three and four: work it, convert

The workspace a user comes back to daily
F06
Live · web

Job Tracker

Every job you scored, applied to, or were sent: with the verdict attached, so your pipeline remembers why.

What it holds
  • Applications with status (saved, applied, interviewing, offer, rejected, withdrawn), dates, location, link and notes
  • The SIGNAL score and decision on the row, and which persona was used
  • A per-job detail page: status, interviews, notes and the job description, all in one place
  • A history of every analysis run, re-openable
Deliberately absent
  • No personal interview-rate dashboards, no score distributions. The team removed them on purpose, because a student staring at their own 12% callback rate is not being helped
  • The counts survive as navigation chips; the scorekeeping does not
A restraint claim worth owning: "we don't grade you, we tell you what to do next." It differentiates sharply from analytics-heavy trackers.
F07
Live · web

Interviews and Prep Now

Every round in one place, and for each one the questions you will actually be asked, with an answer to each built from your own experience.

Tracking the rounds
  • Three groups that sort themselves: Coming up, Waiting to hear, Completed
  • Rounds by stage and format, interviewer names, thank-you tracking, confidence and notes
  • The soonest round takes over the dashboard as the single thing that matters
  • A preparation checklist that re-orders itself inside 24 hours, when there is nothing left to read and everything left to do
Prep Now: the generated pack
  • Where you're exposed. What they will want you to prove, taken from the strengths in your scan, and what they will push on, taken from its risks. Each with one line on how to handle it
  • What they'll ask. Real questions: one per requirement the posting actually names, one per risk in the version an interviewer would say out loud, plus "why this job" and "why you"
  • What you say. A drafted answer to every one of those questions, written in your voice, in second person
  • Under every answer, the lines from your own resume it rests on, so you can check it before you say it
  • Generated on demand behind a button, then frozen. Regenerating with the same inputs returns the same pack rather than a different one
This is the single most defensible AI feature in the product and the brief previously undersold it badly. It is built so that it cannot flatter you: an answer may only use experience the engine actually extracted from your resume, it may not invent a system, a number, a tool or a team name that is not in the evidence, and it knows nothing about the company beyond the posting you gave it. Any answer citing evidence it was not handed is discarded before you see it, and if the whole pack fails those checks the feature shows nothing rather than showing you something to walk into a room with. "Every word is something you can actually back up when they follow up."
F08
Live · web

Networking Tracker

Stop being one of four hundred applications: a relationship CRM that tells you who to contact today and hands you the message already written.

The daily loop
  • A due-today worklist: overdue first, with the reason stated. This is the page, everything else sits under it
  • An eleven-stage pipeline from identified through intro requested, active sequence, replied, chat, nurture, the ask, and outcome
  • A three-touch cadence engine that sets the next due date automatically and moves a silent contact to dormant on its own
  • Resurfacing built in: no-answer contacts return after five weeks, declined after three months
The rest of it
  • 24 message templates covering intro asks, three-touch sequences by relationship type, scheduling, thank-yous, nurture, the ask and LinkedIn notes, each auto-selected for the contact and merge-filled from the user's networking profile
  • Copy and mark sent in one action, which advances the pipeline and schedules the follow-up
  • A company board grouped by tier, including target firms with no contacts yet
  • CSV / Excel import with a preview-and-commit wizard
  • A funnel dashboard with reply and conversation rates, and a weekly target of five to eight new first touches
  • Contacts are linked to applications: a job's detail page shows your network at that company
This is now the whole of SIGNAL's outreach offer, and the most under-told asset in the product. It is a real CRM with a real cadence engine, and it is the function that creates the daily habit a $99 purchase needs in order to feel used. "You don't have to remember to follow up. It tells you."
F09
Live · web

The Adaptive Home

One screen that shows the one or two things that need you, and changes shape as the search changes.

Five states, in priority order
  • New: profile incomplete, with a completion meter; nothing else works without it
  • Interview: a scheduled round ahead takes the whole screen
  • Ready: set up but nothing tracked yet, the get-started state
  • Quiet: real data but nothing touched in a week; re-engagement, warm and without guilt
  • Active: the normal working state, showing what's due, who is awaiting a reply, what has gone stale and this week's counts
Why it exists
  • Built explicitly for the user with no coach: a wall of panels is what an unaccompanied job seeker cannot parse
  • The single "what needs you" surface for the whole app
Retention story in one line: "open it and it tells you what today is for." This is what makes SIGNAL a place rather than a tool, and it is worth showing in product screenshots more than the scan is.
04

Why the verdict is credible

The differentiation claim that matters most is also the least visible: SIGNAL's decision is computed, not opined. This section is the ammunition for that claim.

The engine is deterministic. On the paid path, no language model decides anything about fit. Signals are extracted from the job description and the resume by rule-based detectors, evidence is matched, penalties are applied, and the score maps to a decision band. The same inputs always produce the same verdict. A model is used only to turn those findings into readable English. The free path adds one narrow extra check, which can spot a "right skill, wrong industry" match and reduce how much it counts. If that check errors or times out it is simply skipped, and the user gets the ordinary deterministic result, so a technical failure can never leave someone with a worse score than the rules alone would have given them.

The rules are one person's judgment, written down. Every detector, gate, risk code and scoring weight in the engine was specified by SIGNAL's founder, who also built it: thirty years on the hiring side, ten of those in executive recruitment, and a working practice as a career counselor to college students and early-career professionals. This is not a model trained on scraped postings, and it is not a committee's rubric. It is what one recruiter knows about how a resume actually gets read, encoded so that it runs the same way every time, for every user, at three in the morning.

The evidence is anchored to the user's own words. A "why you're competitive" bullet has to cite a fact from the resume; a positioning edit's before-text must be a literal substring of the resume or the system throws it away; a risk bullet can only be generated for a risk the engine actually raised, and the prompt forbids inventing one. Where a question has a deterministic answer, such as whether a summary section exists or whether a keyword is present, a regex answers it and the model is told the answer as a fact.

The engine has guardrails against flattering the user. A near-perfect score with any medium or high risk attached cannot reach the top band. A case with no high-quality direct evidence is capped at Review no matter how well the keywords line up, and a resume's skills-list tag row explicitly does not count as proof that someone has done the work.

The same discipline governs the generated interview answers. Prep Now may only make claims the engine actually extracted from the user's resume, and it is forbidden from adding a system, a threshold, a team name or a mechanism the evidence does not contain, on the stated test that an interviewer could follow up on any invented detail and the candidate would have nothing to say. It knows nothing about the employer beyond the posting, and its response format has no field for company research, so there is nothing to fill. Every drafted answer must name the evidence it rests on, and one citing evidence it was never handed is dropped. If the whole pack fails those checks the feature writes nothing and tells the user it could not generate, rather than quietly serving a thinner version. That is the right trade here: this is material a person walks into a room and repeats out loud, so getting nothing is recoverable and getting something invented is not.

It is regression-tested like software, not tuned by vibes. Twenty-six hand-built cases run against a frozen baseline on every change, the full suite plus a typecheck runs in CI on every push, and a promotion to production is refused for any commit CI has not passed. Historical production runs are periodically inspected for structural drift.

How to use this in market

The tagline is Stop applying blind, and everything in this section is the proof behind it. Do not lead with "AI." Every competitor leads with AI, and the category's credibility problem is invention. Lead instead with judgment you can audit: it shows its work, it cites your resume, it refuses to make things up, and it will tell you not to apply.

Always attach the provenance, because it is the part no competitor can copy. The reason SIGNAL can say "don't apply" and be believed is that a named person wrote the rules and built the system: thirty years on the hiring side, ten in executive recruitment, and years counseling students and early-career professionals through exactly this. Anyone can license the same models. Nobody else has that source. The engine is not a substitute for that expertise, it is that expertise made repeatable and available at scale.

Pricing tension, currently under evaluation

SIGNAL sells one thing today: $99 once, with no expiry. Buy it and you keep access. The product's centre of gravity has moved underneath that arrangement. The scan is a single act and a one-time fee suits it well, but the tracker, the networking cadence engine and the adaptive home are habits, and a habit carries an ongoing cost to serve. A perpetual one-time fee monetizes the first and gives the second away forever.

The audience is also seasonal, which cuts both ways. A graduating senior's search has a natural beginning and end, so a term price matches the shape of the need better than a perpetual licence does. But the same seasonality means a subscription will be cancelled the moment someone lands, which is the outcome the product is built to produce, so the model has to be priced against a short and successful life rather than a long one.

Fix first: the live copy undersells what you deliver

The free-scan results page tells users that full access is "unlimited JobFit analyses for 3 months," and the API sends a matching "3 months" term string alongside the price. Nothing in the system enforces that. purchase_date is read only for the 7-day refund window and a "Member since" line, and access is gated on an active flag that flips false only on a refund.

So the product currently advertises less than it grants. Resolve it in one direction or the other before any pricing test runs, because a price test is unreadable while the control describes a term the system does not apply.

Worth closing before the next acquisition push.

07

Honest boundaries

What SIGNAL deliberately does not do. Knowing these prevents a campaign promise the product will have to walk back.

  • It does not apply for you. No auto-apply, no bulk submission. That is a positioning choice, not a gap: the entire thesis is that indiscriminate volume is the problem.
  • It does not send your messages. Outreach is copy-and-paste by design; the user's own inbox and LinkedIn stay the channel. The tracker records that you sent it.
  • It does not host your documents. Files live where they already live; SIGNAL indexes and links.
  • It cannot always read a posting from a URL. URL parsing works across most applicant tracking systems, but LinkedIn in particular blocks it: pasting the text is the reliable path, and the copy should keep saying so.
  • The free scan analyzes; it does not remember. Nothing is tracked until the user has an account.
The one claim to keep tightly worded

SIGNAL scores fit against a job description. It does not see the other applicants, the hiring manager's actual preferences, or the internal candidate who was always going to get it. "Your real odds on each one" is defensible as shorthand for evidence-based fit; anything that implies knowledge of the applicant pool is not.

08

Angles worth testing

Five positions the product can genuinely support, ordered by how much of the build they put to work.

A

Stop applying blind

The tagline. "Know if it's worth applying, before you waste the Saturday."

The current lead and the right one for cold traffic. Highest-contrast against every competitor, it names the reader's actual behaviour rather than the product's category, and the free scan delivers on it in thirty seconds. Its one weakness is that it sells a moment rather than a method, which is what makes the $99 ask feel large at the point of decision.

B

The honest one

"Nothing invented. Every line is something you actually did."

Aimed squarely at the anxiety AI resume tools create. Uniquely defensible here because the constraint is enforced in code, not in a prompt. Strong for parents, career centers and any institutional channel.

C

The system, not the tool

"Open it and it tells you what today is for."

Puts the adaptive home, the tracker, the networking cadence engine and Prep Now to work: the two-thirds of the build the current copy barely mentions. This is the retention story and the one that justifies a recurring price. Prep Now in particular is the moment the product proves its worth, because it arrives exactly when the stakes are highest and it is the hardest thing here to replicate.

D

Get a human to vouch for you

"A referral gets you read instead of filtered."

Networking is the highest-anxiety, lowest-competence area for the target user, and SIGNAL is unusually deep here: a due-today worklist, a cadence engine that schedules every follow-up, two dozen written templates, and contacts linked to the jobs you scored. Nobody else in the student segment carries a real CRM for this.

E

What a career coach would tell you

"Thirty years on the hiring side. Ten in executive recruitment. Now it will read your resume the way they do."

Sells the judgment rather than the software, which suits an audience that distrusts the software. Strongest in institutional and referral channels, where a named practitioner travels further than a feature list, and it is the angle that makes a $99 ask feel like access to expertise rather than a subscription to a tool.

Recommendation

Keep A as the acquisition hook: it converts cold traffic and the free scan proves it in thirty seconds. Move C into the post-scan and upgrade surfaces, where the job is no longer to earn attention but to justify $99; today those surfaces re-argue the scan instead of showing the workspace. Use B as the trust layer running through all of it.
